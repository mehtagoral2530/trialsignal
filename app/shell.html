    About TrialSignal

    Medical studies are public, but they’re written for experts. TrialSignal turns them into plain answers anyone can follow.

    Why it exists

    When you hear about a new treatment, the facts are spread across a government study registry, a scientific paper, a company press release and a regulator’s decision. Each is written for a different reader. TrialSignal brings them together and answers the same simple questions every time: what was tested, who took part, what they found, whether it was safe, and how sure we can be.

    Who it’s for

    Anyone curious about a medical study, including patients and families, students, and people starting a career in health or research.

    What it doesn’t do

    • It doesn’t tell you whether a treatment is right for you. Talk to your doctor.
    • It doesn’t decide whether you could join a study. Only the study team can.
    • It doesn’t say one treatment is better than another unless a study compared them directly.

    Where the information comes from

    Study details come from ClinicalTrials.gov, the U.S. government’s public registry, and update live when it can be reached. Results come from peer-reviewed medical journals, linked on each study. News marked “Picked by hand” comes from FDA announcements and company releases. Other news is written automatically from ClinicalTrials.gov and FDA records, checked against PubMed, Europe PMC, RxNorm and DailyMed, and each story lists its sources. The plain-English study summaries and hand-picked news are written by hand from those sources.
